## Does leaning on the heuristic less mean agreeing with the annotators more?

The disputed rows are the ones the four frontier models got wrong when asked for a label alone (script 26), so their accuracy there is near zero by construction and only the open models give a usable reading. It is not exactly zero because these columns read the second run, where the same models were asked for a label *and* a reason (script 35), and a model does not always repeat itself: the frontier models land on a different label than they did in script 26 on 2%-10% of the disputed rows and 1%-12% of the control rows. That instability is small next to the effect being measured, and it is reported rather than hidden because the groups were fixed by the first run.

The control column is the check that matters: a model that simply says `Clear` more often would score better on the disputed rows and worse on the control ones.

## Reading

A replication here removes the most economical objection to the mechanism argument, which is that four models from three vendors may still share enough provenance to share one habit. A model an order of magnitude smaller, trained by a fourth party, with open weights, is a different kind of witness.

If the asymmetry does not replicate, that is equally worth having: it would place the behaviour in the frontier training recipe rather than in the task, and the paper's claim would have to narrow accordingly.
