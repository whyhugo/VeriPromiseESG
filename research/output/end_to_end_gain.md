## Reading

**The S4 gain does not survive the cascade.** On gold-conditioned rows the
dedicated head scored 0.7104 against 0.6602 for the competition ensemble. Gated
on *predicted* `evidence_status` it makes the official score slightly worse,
0.6384 to 0.6368. S4 was measuring an easier problem than the one that counts.

The per-class table shows exactly where it goes:

* `N/A` improves, 0.6913 to 0.7075, because the structural gate derives it from
  `evidence_status` instead of predicting it.
* `Not Clear` degrades, 0.3556 to 0.3223, because the gate removes an escape
  hatch. When the `evidence_status` head is wrong, the joint four-class quality
  head can still emit a different label and be right; the cascade cannot.

## Why enforcing the hierarchy costs macro-F1

Splitting the 2,000 test rows by whether `evidence_status` was predicted
correctly (checked separately against the competition submission):

| | n | quality correct as-is | quality correct if forced consistent |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `evidence_status` correct | 1603 | 0.848 | **0.867** |
| `evidence_status` wrong | 397 | **0.176** | 0.121 |

Forcing helps on the 80% where the upstream head is right and hurts on the 20%
where it is wrong, and on raw accuracy the trade is roughly break-even. Macro-F1
is not accuracy. Forcing shifts mass toward `N/A`, the second-largest class,
which lifts `N/A` F1 while costing recall on `Not Clear`, and macro-averaging
weights the small class equally.

So the mechanism behind the negative results in script 05 (post-hoc repair,
-0.003 to -0.005) and here is the same one: **under macro-F1 with imbalanced
classes, enforcing a label hierarchy systematically trades rare-class recall for
majority-class precision, and the metric punishes that trade.** Structural
consistency is not free, and the usual intuition that adding valid constraints
can only help is wrong under this metric.

The concrete hedging cases are visible too. Among rows where `evidence_status`
was wrongly predicted `No` or `N/A`, there are 13 where the quality head still
emitted `Not Clear` and was right; the cascade turns all of them into `N/A`.
Among the 232 rows wrongly predicted `Yes`, only 9 (3.9%) had the quality head
correctly holding out for `N/A`, so that direction of the hedge is small.
