## Reading

Every stratum with more than a handful of observations lands between 0.70 and 0.87, and the intervals overlap. It does not matter whether the paragraph is one the annotators disagreed about, which verdict the model reached, which vendor built it, or whether it is a frontier system or an open 8B one. The one stratum that looks lower -- rows the models called `Clear` -- rests on a handful of observations and its interval spans most of the unit line, so it distinguishes nothing.

That is the point. A quantity this stable under stratification is a property of how these models read the criterion rather than of the sample. When one of them says a paragraph is vague, roughly five times in six the same sentence says what is missing is numbers.

**This replaces the disputed-versus-control comparison in script 35.** That comparison put 0.149 against 0.714 with a vanishing p-value, but the control group is overwhelmingly rows the models called `Clear` and the disputed group overwhelmingly rows they called `Not Clear`. Conditioned on the verdict the difference disappears (script 48), so the test was measuring that models cite missing figures when they answer `Not Clear` -- true, and close to a tautology. The co-occurrence above needs no group split and makes the stronger claim directly.
