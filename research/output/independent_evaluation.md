# An evaluation framework independent of the competition weighting

Generated by `research/scripts/16_independent_evaluation.py` on the official 2,000-row test set.

## Why this exists

The official formula is `0.20*PS + 0.30*ES + 0.35*EQ + 0.15*VT`, all macro-F1. Those weights are a competition design choice, and some of our earlier claims inherit them: "Misleading decides 8.75 points" is just `0.35 x 0.25`. A weighting chosen to shape a leaderboard is not a measuring instrument for a paper.

All three NTCIR-18 AI CUP special-session papers take the same line -- they use the competition dataset as material and then define discipline-standard metrics for their own question (MRR@10, NDCG@10, Recall@100, AP@1), with an explicit baseline, an approximate upper bound, ablations, and significance testing. None reports a leaderboard score as the paper's evidence.

## How much leverage does a 3-instance class really have?

| aggregation | value with Misleading | value ignoring Misleading | difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| macro-F1 | 0.4642 | 0.6189 | +0.1547 |
| support-weighted F1 | 0.7179 | 0.7189 | +0.0011 |
| balanced accuracy | 0.4666 | 0.6222 | +0.1555 |

A class with 3 of 2,000 instances moves macro-F1 by a quarter of the subtask while moving support-weighted F1 and accuracy by almost nothing. That gap is the argument: macro-averaging is not defensible when a class is this degenerate, regardless of what weight the competition puts on the subtask.

## Reading

**The ranking is not stable, and our own submission choice was wrong under the
metric that matters most.** `ens_3way_tuned` is first under the competition
formula and **last** under joint exact match (0.4880 against 0.5130 for
`ens_3way_raw`). The "tuning" was per-class threshold tuning to maximise
macro-F1; it bought +0.0022 on the competition total and cost -0.0250 on the
fraction of paragraphs that come out completely right. An auditor using the
system would prefer the untuned model.

**Normalising to the human ceiling changes which subtask looks hard.** Under
competition macro-F1, `evidence_quality` (0.4642) looks far worse than
`verification_timeline` (0.6545) -- a gap of 0.19. Measured as agreement with
the gold labels relative to the agreement the annotators reached with each
other, they are almost the same: **0.530 against 0.547**. The competition metric
exaggerates how uniquely bad `evidence_quality` is, because that subtask happens
to carry a class with three instances and macro-averaging gives it a quarter of
the weight. No model reaches even two thirds of the human ceiling on any
subtask.

**The conditional view shows the headline numbers are partly gating, not
judgement.** Scored only on rows where the gold upstream label makes the subtask
applicable, `evidence_status` rises 0.7132 -> 0.7500 and `verification_timeline`
0.6545 -> 0.6710, because a large share of their unconditional score is just
correctly propagating "this paragraph contains no promise". `evidence_quality`
moves the other way, 0.4642 -> 0.4339: for that subtask the `N/A` rows were the
easy part, and the judgement itself is harder than the headline suggests.

The same view replaces the structural-consistency index we had been quoting with
something directly interpretable: the system emits a label the guideline makes
impossible on 80 rows for `evidence_status`, 115 for `evidence_quality` and 59
for `verification_timeline`.

**The three-instance class argument, stated without competition weights.**
Dropping `Misleading` from the average moves macro-F1 by **+0.1547** and
support-weighted F1 by **+0.0011**, a factor of about 140. That is the whole
case: macro-averaging is indefensible when one class has 3 of 2,000 instances,
and the conclusion does not depend on the subtask carrying a weight of 0.35, or
any weight at all.

**Trivial baselines are not trivial here.** Always predicting the majority label
gets 29.2% of paragraphs completely right, against 48.8-51.3% for the trained
systems. Any claim of progress has to be read against that.

## What this framework recommends reporting

1. All four subtasks separately, never collapsed into a single weighted number.
2. Per-class F1 **with support**, so degenerate classes are visible.
3. Balanced accuracy and MCC alongside macro-F1, since they behave sensibly
   under this level of imbalance.
4. Joint exact match, as the quantity an application actually needs.
5. Agreement relative to the reported inter-annotator ceiling, which makes
   subtasks with different label-set sizes comparable.
6. Bootstrap intervals on 2,000 rows, since differences of 0.002 between
   submissions are inside the noise.
