## Reading

**`evidence_quality` saturates at epoch 3 and never moves again.** It reaches
0.501 on the third epoch and finishes at 0.505 on the fifteenth -- **+0.0049 for
twelve more epochs**. Every other subtask keeps climbing over the same span:
`verification_timeline` +0.0383, `evidence_status` +0.0277.

That contrast is the point. Three of the four subtasks are still limited by
optimisation at epoch 4; `evidence_quality` is not limited by optimisation at
all past epoch 3. Whatever separates `Clear` from `Not Clear` in this corpus,
the model has extracted all of it almost immediately, and further training
finds nothing more.

This is what "we could not improve subtask three" has to rest on to be a finding
rather than an admission, and it now lines up with everything else:

| approach | result |
|---|---|
| more training (this script) | +0.0049 over 12 epochs |
| more data for the rare class (script 19) | worse in every condition |
| a lower bar for predicting it (script 18) | monotone loss, no interior optimum |
| span annotations as input (script 07) | +0.008, CI crosses zero |
| span annotations as a relation (script 09) | AUC 0.4671, below chance |
| dedicated instead of joint heads (script 15) | 0.4246 vs 0.4472, no gain |

Six independent interventions, none of which moves it. The subtask is not
under-optimised, under-trained, under-parameterised or badly thresholded.

**Training budget explains only a small part of the gap to the competition
ensemble.** Going from 4 to 15 epochs is worth **+0.0039** on the official
total. The gap script 15 left open was 0.6384 - 0.6157 = 0.0230, so after
accounting for budget about 0.019 remains, and script 15 attributes +0.0142 of
that to ensembling three backbones instead of one. Budget plus ensembling
covers roughly 0.018 of 0.023, leaving little for the joint-versus-dedicated
formulation -- consistent with script 15 finding the two formulations identical
at 0.6015 apiece.

**Best-epoch selection is not better than just training longer.** Selecting per
subtask on out-of-fold validation gives 0.6143 against 0.6154 for a flat 15
epochs. With curves this flat past epoch 8, the selection is fitting validation
noise.

**One caveat on the `fixed 4 epochs` row.** It is the epoch-4 checkpoint of a
15-epoch OneCycle schedule, so its learning rate is still near peak, whereas
script 15's 4-epoch runs annealed fully within 4 epochs. The two are not the
same model and should not be differenced against each other; 0.6115 here versus
0.6015 there is mostly that difference. The epoch-to-epoch comparisons *within*
this table share one schedule and are sound.
