## Additivity check

| effect | measured at 1 backbone | measured at 3 backbones |
|---|---:|---:|
| enforcing the hierarchy | +0.0013 | +0.0003 |
| ensembling (1 -> 3 backbones) | +0.0142 (free) | +0.0132 (projected) |

Script 14 measured the hierarchy cost at -0.0130 on the joint formulation with one backbone. If the effect is stable across formulation and ensembling, the two columns above should agree with it and with each other.

Extrapolated target from script 14 was 0.649 for the `dedicated, 3 backbones, free` cell. Measured: **0.6157**.

## Reading

**The additivity assumption fails, and with it the extrapolation.** Script 14
separated three effects and I projected their sum to 0.649. The combination
measures **0.6157**, short by 0.033. None of the three effects survived at the
size it was measured at.

**The hierarchy cost does not replicate on this formulation.** Script 14 found
projection onto the feasible label set costing **-0.0130** with identical
weights. Here the same operation costs **+0.0013** at one backbone and
**+0.0003** at three -- zero, and if anything the wrong sign. So that effect is
specific to the joint four-head model, not a property of the task or the metric.
The three earlier measurements agreed with each other because all three were run
on joint or cascaded architectures; none of them was an independent replication.

Why the difference is plausible: the joint model shares one encoder, so its four
heads are correlated and a violation usually means the shared representation was
wrong for the whole row -- projecting then propagates that error. Four
independently trained models are miscalibrated independently, so a violation is
more often one head being wrong while the others are right, which is exactly the
case projection repairs.

**Dedicated models give nothing over joint ones, except on
`verification_timeline`.** At one backbone with free composition the two
formulations score **identically**, 0.6015 against 0.6015. The only subtask that
moves is `verification_timeline`, 0.5291 -> 0.5848, **+0.0557**. That direction
matches script 14's +0.0952 on the same subtask, so it is the one finding here
that has now replicated across two independent runs, at roughly half the
originally measured size.

**Ensembling is worth about half what was estimated.** +0.0142 rather than the
~+0.03 read off script 13 against the competition submission -- that earlier
figure was contaminated by a training-budget difference, not just backbone count.

**Nothing built here beats the competition ensemble (0.6384).** The best new
configuration reaches 0.6160. One confound matters for that comparison and not
for the others: the competition system trained 15 epochs with best-epoch
selection on validation, while every run in scripts 13-15 uses a fixed 4 epochs.
All the internal contrasts above are matched on that budget; only the comparison
against 0.6384 is not.
