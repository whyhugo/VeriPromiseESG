## Reading

The two tests turn out to measure different things, and separating them is more informative than the single axis this script was written to look for.

**Test 1 measures difficulty, not divergence.** Verbatim scores: +0.298 (promise_status), +0.375 (evidence_status), +0.002 (evidence_quality), +0.210 (verification_timeline). The guideline's literal words are worth almost nothing on `evidence_quality` -- the two phrases it offers occur nowhere -- and the jump to a working operationalisation is +0.375, the largest of the four. For the other three the guideline's own words already carry most of what the extension adds.

**Test 2 measures divergence.** Gaps: +0.041 (promise_status), +0.171 (evidence_status), +0.246 (evidence_quality), +0.001 (verification_timeline). `evidence_quality` is the largest at +0.246 and `verification_timeline` is essentially zero.

Putting the axes together separates the subtasks in a way a single difficulty score does not:

| subtask | hard? | model and annotator diverge? | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 `promise_status` | no | no | solved; α 0.977 |
| 2 `evidence_status` | moderately | somewhat | tractable |
| 4 `verification_timeline` | **yes** | **no** | hard, but model and annotator fail *together* |
| 3 `evidence_quality` | yes | **yes** | hard *and* the two parties are not answering the same question |

`verification_timeline` is the control that matters. It is at least as hard as `evidence_quality` by Test 1, yet its divergence is zero: the system and the annotators are reading the same surface evidence and both finding it insufficient. That is ordinary difficulty, and ordinary difficulty responds to ordinary remedies -- which is exactly what the organisers' own baseline shows, where retrieval lifted `verification_timeline` from 0.3090 to 0.4227 while moving `evidence_quality` only from 0.3549 to 0.3599.

So the claim this script supports is narrower than the one it set out to test, and better for being narrower: **`evidence_quality` is not simply the hardest subtask -- it is the only one where the difficulty is a disagreement about the question rather than about the answer.** Extra data, retrieval and larger models address the second kind of difficulty and not the first, which is why they have worked on subtask 4 and not on subtask 3.
