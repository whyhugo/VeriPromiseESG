# Is the divergence pattern statistically real?

Generated by `research/scripts/33_divergence_significance.py`. Two groups: the 595 rows where all four frontier models agree **and match** the gold label, and the 147 where they agree **and all four differ** from it.

## A correction to how these features were described

Earlier scripts called these "the criteria the guideline lists". That was wrong, and the error is worth stating plainly.

No released document contains that list. The seven patterns below are **this project's own operationalisation**, arrived at by observing what correlates with the label in the training data, not by transcribing a published rule. What follows is therefore a comparison against *our* notion of concreteness, and is labelled as such throughout.

`research/scripts/34_guideline_criteria_test.py` follows the error through: it sets out what the two released documents actually say, and measures whether the criteria they state, or the ones used here, better explain the models and the annotators. That is the fuller account.

**6 of 7 features survive Holm-Bonferroni correction at the 0.05 level.** Every surviving difference is negative: the rows the models get wrong together carry fewer of these markers.

## Reading

The pattern is not sampling noise. The differences are large, the intervals exclude zero, and they survive correction for testing seven features at once.

What that establishes is narrow but solid: **the paragraphs on which four independently built frontier models agree with each other and differ from the annotators are systematically the ones carrying fewer surface markers of concreteness.** It does not establish that the models are wrong, nor that the annotators are; script 31 addressed that separately and concluded that the guideline text does not determine the label either way.
