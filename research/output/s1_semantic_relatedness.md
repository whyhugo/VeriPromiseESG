## Reading

H5 -- that the two gold spans support a relatedness score which predicts quality
-- is refuted.

- Both dense scores are at or below chance on Clear vs Not Clear: bi-encoder
  cosine AUC **0.4671**, cross-encoder reranker **0.5095**. The bi-encoder is
  *below* chance, meaning Not Clear evidence is slightly more similar to its
  promise than Clear evidence is.
- This agrees in direction with the lexical result in script 08 (Not Clear had
  higher char-overlap with the promise, 0.1049 vs 0.0747). Two independent
  measures, one lexical and one dense, say the same thing: **superficial
  evidence restates the promise; substantive evidence introduces new content.**
- Relatedness does not find the Misleading instances. Their cosine percentiles
  are 54.9, 10.8, 72.8, 8.5 and 90.7 -- one of them is among the *most* related
  rows in the corpus. A zero-shot relatedness detector for Misleading does not
  work.
- Adding all three relatedness scores to the guideline features moves test
  macro-F1 from 0.6512 to 0.6597, which is inside the noise band on n=1332.

What survives is `len_ratio`, at AUC 0.6542, and it is identical lexically and
semantically (0.6535 vs 0.6542) -- so it is a length effect, not a semantic one.
Taken together with S2, the operative construct is **elaboration**, not
relatedness: Clear evidence is longer than its promise and carries specificity
markers, Not Clear evidence is short and restates.
