## Reading

Two predictions were on the table before this ran. `cot` should not help, because the failure is not a shortage of reasoning -- the direct reasons were already long, specific and agreed across vendors. `decomp` should loosen the collapse, because it makes the model commit to each stated criterion before it can reach for a label.

**The first prediction holds.** Step-by-step reasoning does not reduce the collapse in either model, and on one of them it tightens it. More reasoning budget spent inside the same heuristic produces more confident application of that heuristic, not escape from it.

**The second has to be read against the distribution table, and only one model gives a clean answer.** Where the label distribution is stable across conditions, the mechanism numbers are comparable and the movement is small: the collapse loosens a little and buys almost no accuracy on the disputed rows. Where the distribution shifts -- a model that answers `Clear` on half the rows under `direct` and much less often under `decomp` -- the mechanism numbers fall for a reason that has nothing to do with the criterion: the model is simply making a different call, and its reasons change with it. That case cannot be counted as the intervention working, and its accuracy on the control rows falls at the same time.

So the defensible conclusion is the harder one. **The four criteria can be put to the model one at a time, in the guideline's own words, and it will still answer them by looking for figures.** The clearest evidence is in the sub-answers themselves: within a single decomposed response, the question about hedging and the question about the link to the promise -- two different criteria -- are both answered by naming missing quantities.

That moves the claim past prompt format. The substitution is not an artefact of asking one question instead of four; it survives being asked about directly, which locates it in how the model represents the criterion rather than in how the criterion was requested.
