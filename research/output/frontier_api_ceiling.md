## Reading

**The four models are unanimous, and unanimously not the annotators.** Three
providers, four independently trained systems, and they agree row for row: two
of the three are `Not Clear`, one is `Clear`. That is a much stronger result
than four independent failures would have been. These models are reading the
paragraphs consistently; they are simply not reading them the way the annotators
did.

**They are not refusing the label.** Between them they emitted `Misleading` 18
times across the 1,335 rows -- opus-5 seven times, gpt-5.6 five, gemini four,
sonnet two -- and every one of those was wrong. The class is understood and
used; it just never lands where the gold standard puts it. That rules out the
most obvious explanation, that the definition was not conveyed.

**Frontier zero-shot loses to a fine-tuned encoder.** The best API model reaches
0.4489 macro-F1 on this three-class problem against 0.6602 for our
three-backbone ensemble on the binary part. Scale and recency do not substitute
for supervision here.

**More capable is worse on this task.** claude-opus-5 is last of the four at
0.3538, a full 0.095 behind claude-sonnet-5, and the loss is concentrated in
`Clear` (0.6721 against 0.8649). It also flagged `Misleading` most often. The
pattern is that stronger reasoning makes a model more willing to find fault with
evidence, which moves it away from annotators who mostly did not. The same shape
appeared locally, where Qwen3-32B scored best alone but agreed most with the
encoder and so contributed least to a vote.

**What this settles.** The claim was previously "nothing we could run finds
`Misleading`", limited by a single consumer GPU. It is now "no method available,
including the current frontier models from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google, finds
it even once, while trained human annotators reached Krippendorff's alpha 0.940
on the same field." Eight and a quarter points of the official score rest on
three rows that nothing can identify -- a property of the evaluation design, not
of any particular system's capacity.
