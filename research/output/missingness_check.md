## 2. Anything that looks significant, tested properly

The concreteness cues are heavy-tailed counts. A z-test on their means assumes a normal sampling distribution that a variable with a long right tail does not have, so it over-rejects. Every comparison above with |z| > 1.96 is re-tested here by permutation, on the median as well as the mean.

| quantity | median: excluded / scored | diff | perm. p | mean diff | perm. p | Bonferroni |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| cue: a percentage | 0.00 / 0.00 | +0.000 | **1.0000** | -0.080 | 0.0891 | 1.000 |
| cue: digits per 100 characters | 2.85 / 3.31 | -0.455 | **0.4010** | -1.268 | 0.0219 | 0.285 |

## 3. Are the failures a batch?

The 80 failures are spread over **39 of the 50 companies** in the evaluation set, and over the id range 12019--13969 with a longest run of consecutive ids of 2. Per-company failure rates run 2.4%--25.0%, and the highest rates sit on the smallest denominators.

A contiguous batch would have meant a few companies or a few minutes of wall clock dropping out together, and a company is a writing style. That is not the shape of these failures.

## Reading

The excluded rows look like the rows that were scored. The gold label distribution is indistinguishable, the lengths are indistinguishable, the failures are scattered across most companies rather than batched, and the three models that did answer them behaved on them as they did elsewhere.

One cue -- digit density -- reaches |z| > 1.96 on a mean comparison and does not survive being tested correctly: the medians are close, the permutation p on the median is far from significant, and the permutation p on the mean does not survive correction for the number of comparisons made. It is the tail of a skewed variable, not a difference in the rows.

**So the exclusion is safe, and re-running the failed calls would cost more than it buys.** The 80 rows would have to be queried now, months after the other 1,255, from a commercial endpoint whose version is not pinned. Script 43 measures how much that matters: re-querying the same model on the same rows changes 2%--12% of its labels. Filling an ignorable gap by mixing two model snapshots into one column would introduce a real inconsistency to remove a hypothetical one.
