## 5. Robustness of the C1 refutation

If `within_2_years` were rare only because a handful of companies dominate the corpus, the refutation would be an artefact of company weighting. Per-company rates (among promise-bearing instances, excluding `already`):

- Companies with >=1 promise-bearing instance in the three real horizons: **49**
- Companies with **at least one** `within_2_years` instance: **21** (43%)
- Per-company `within_2_years` rate: median **0.0%**, mean **4.7%**, max **50.0%** (ticker `3034`)
- Companies exceeding the ML-Promise Semiconductor figure of 59.87%: **0**

## 6. Threats to validity

**T1. Differential paragraph sampling (the main confound).** VeriPromiseESG4K shows an 81.3% promise rate against 40.2% in ML-Promise, which is consistent with the competition corpus having preferentially sampled promise-bearing paragraphs. This alone could explain the Promise-Identification and Actionable-Evidence gaps and they should not be read as evidence about corporate behaviour.

  _However, it does not explain the timing result._ The `within_2_years` comparison is computed **conditional on a promise existing** in both corpora, so over-sampling promise-bearing paragraphs leaves it unaffected. The C1 refutation is therefore substantially more robust than the C3/C4 ones.

**T2. Small expected cells in the goodness-of-fit tests.** The Semiconductor timing GOF is dominated by the `between_2_and_5_years` cell, whose ML-Promise proportion of 1.27% yields an expected count of ~1.4 against 63 observed. That is below the conventional expected-count-of-5 threshold, so the chi2 magnitude is inflated even though the direction and effect size are unambiguous. Report the effect size, not the statistic.

**T3. Single-company industry cells.** The Energy and Biomedical groups contain one company each (27 instances). Only the Semiconductor comparison (7 companies, 305 instances) supports an industry-level claim.

**T4. Label-vocabulary drift.** ML-Promise uses `Other` where VeriPromiseESG4K uses `already`; the guideline text for both is 'already verified or no specific timing'. Their shares are close (37.5% vs 44.1%), so the timing shift is a movement *between the three real horizons*, not an artefact of this rename.

**T5. Annotator and guideline revision.** The two corpora were annotated by different teams. ML-Promise reports Cohen's kappa 0.62-0.80 for CPEP and 0.60-0.89 for TV; no agreement figures have been published for VeriPromiseESG4K. Part of the gap may be annotator drift rather than corpus content, which is itself a finding worth reporting.

**T6. Market-cap snapshot.** Tiers use a current TWSE price snapshot, not the value at 2024 report publication. Tier boundaries could shift for companies near a tertile edge.

## 7. Data-quality observations surfaced by this comparison

**(a) The two labelled splits encode the not-applicable state differently.**

| split | promise_status=No rows | `evidence_status` surface form |
|---|---:|---|
| train | 186 | '' |
| val | 187 | 'N/A' |

The official `sample_submission_format.csv` uses `N/A`. A model trained directly on the train split without normalisation emits the empty string and scores zero on every not-applicable row. The same split applies to `verification_timeline` and `evidence_quality`.

**(b) The nominal 50 companies are 49.** 
Ticker `3231` appears under 2 name variants (`Wistron|wistron`), inflating a naive company count to 50.

**(c) `esg_type` multi-labels use inconsistent orderings.** 11 distinct multi-label strings covering 114 instances: `E;G`=9, `E;S`=23, `E;S;G`=26, `G;E`=3, `G;E;S`=2, `G;S`=10, `G;S;E`=5, `S;E`=12, `S;E;G`=2, `S;G`=21, `S;G;E`=1. Empty: 21.

**(d) `Misleading` support is n=2** in VeriPromiseESG4K and n=0 in the ML-Promise Chinese subset (10 across all five ML-Promise languages combined). It carries 1/4 of the `evidence_quality` macro-F1 weight, which itself is 35% of the official score.
